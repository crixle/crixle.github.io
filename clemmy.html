Clementine

June 2019 - March 2024

Summary

As we approach the 2 year anniversary of the passing of my late bearded dragon, Clementine, I wanted to create a dedication for her so her life and her memory lives on. While this hidden post is located on a small, (very) low-traffic blog, I was hoping in some way this would get archived even if this blog ceases to exist. I have fought with myself on whether this was necessary or even pathetic, but grieving is weird and this is helping me cope with her passing even after all the time has passed.

The Beginning

I got Clem when I was 18, right before I moved out of my parents house - to my parent's dismay. I normally don't like lizards or any animal that isn't furry, but bearded dragons have a soft spot in my heart for some reason. It's something about their personalities and their expressive faces.

When I moved out into my first apartment, I snuck her in with me. The lease didn't even allow cats or dogs, let alone a lizard. I was working part-time making $14/hr and barely had enough money to pay for food and my rent - but I always had to find a way to pay for her crickets and roaches (they're expensive!). Over the 4 months I lived in that apartment, she was held against me by my roommate who threatened to tell our landlord if I didn't do X, Y, or Z.

I eventually broke the lease and told my landlord about her before the roommate could (fuck you, Shania). We moved back into my parents house right as COVID-19 hit. We had a lot of time to hang out together, and during that very drunken phase of my life she was my best friend and would hang out next to me 24/7.

Her expressions, while subtle and seemingly ingenuine, were always so expressive and she always seemed to tell a story. It's hard to explain.

Remodel

After a few months of living with my parents, I eventually met my current husband and started to move into his house. Clemmy came first. I took the opportunity to finally upgrade her tank from a 20G to a 40G with a bunch of new decor pieces and fixtures for her to lounge on. She loved it. She had a side of her tank with direct sunlight on it most of the day and that's where she spent most of her time.

When I met my husband, he had a dog and a cat who were both very interested in Clementine and both would just sit next to her tank and watch her for hours. I did take her out a few time under close supervision and let them meet each other - black beard immediately. Never again. I would let Clem stretch her legs a bit by letting her run around the house when I knew neither of them were around.

Over the next couple years, I would continuously improve her life with more stuff to enrich her environment and prepped an emergency egg-laying bin since it was possible for her to bare eggs at anytime - even if they were unfertile. I wanted to make sure I was prepared and she was comfortable.

Winters

Winters in my state are rough. Normally in the single digits or teens, super dry, and we'd get storms that would take out the power for hours or days at a time. That didn't work well with a cold-blooded lizard. We had an emergency generator that was only capable of running a few devices at once and one of them was always a heat lamp for her. I would run a cable from outside into her tank and insulate it with a bunch of blankets so she stayed toasty even when we didn't have power.

The Final Act

In November of 2023, Clementine stopped eating bugs or any of her greens. This was common for bearded dragons to an extent (cold weather = brumation), but this time was different. Weeks passed and the only thing that she was interested in was blueberries (her absolute favorite) and she would eat maybe 2 or 3 roaches every week, but that can't sustain a lizard. Our normal vet was out on maternity leave and I couldn't get her seen for months. Finally, in January I eventually took her to another vet a few hours away and they examined her and ran some fecal tests. Came back fine. Nothing was wrong from their perspective. They recommended that I syringe-feed her an emergency liquid diet to get some nutrients in her and that was traumatizing for the both of us. She didn't like me trying to shove a dull syringe in her mouth every day. But, it was working. She was visibly more active, more alert, and I felt hope.

After a couple more weeks, she still wasn't interested in bugs or greens and I was still syringe feeding her. I woke up one morning and she was out of it. She had excreted something yellow all over her tank and wasn't responsive to me touching her or picking her up. Putting her in warm water made her wake up for a few moments to just bob her head a few time but then she'd ragdoll again. The pads on her head had shrunked and were sinking into her head which made me believe she was very dehydrated so I tried syringing her little bits of water at a time and let her rest.

The next morning, Clementine wasn't responsive, period. Nothing could get her to move but she was still breathing. After a few more hours, Clementine passed away in my presense - her daddy was right there in her final moments.

In Conclusion

I have struggled with Clem's passing for almost 2 years now. I keep thinking of what I could've done differently - if I could've intervened sooner - if I did this, that, and the other. I try to tell myself I did the best I could - even if that wasn't good enough.

She's in a better place. A large overgrown field with dandelions everywhere and it's sunny every day. She can chase bugs around and stop for a quick dandelion snack right before perching on a nice hot rock to bask in the sun.

There's a part of me that's relieved. I no longer have to worry about her well-being, if she's warm, if she's fed, if she shit all over her tank, etc. I would worry about all of those obsessively when I wasn't home or if she was under the care of someone else.

I've thought about getting another bearded dragon, but no other one could compare to her or replace her. Also, bearded dragons are exotic animals that need to live in their natural habitats. There's not domesticated to rely on humans like cats and dogs, they were essentially plucked from their homes in Austrailia and we force them to live in glass tanks their whole lives. That's not fulfilling for them. They deserve to live long, healthy, and free lives in their natural habitats.

In my opinion, I provided impeccable husbandry for her. I researched the shit out of it and made sure her quality of life was spectacular. My heart hurts for all the beardies that get put in the care of a disinterested 7 year old and their parents who just put them in a cold tank with a red light and sand.

Final Remark

I love you, Clementine. You're daddy's girl. I miss you like crazy and I will for the rest of my life. Keep chasing crickets up there and we'll see each other again some day. ❤️
